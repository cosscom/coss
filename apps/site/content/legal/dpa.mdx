This Data Processing Addendum ("**DPA**") forms part of, and is incorporated by reference into, the
[Terms of Service](/legal/terms) or any other written agreement for coss.com (the "**Agreement**") between **Cal.com,
Inc.**, 2261 Market Street #4382, San Francisco, CA 94114, United States ("**Cal.com**"), and the customer that is party
to the Agreement ("**Customer**"). It applies automatically, with no signature needed, whenever Cal.com processes
Customer Personal Data in providing the Services. Customers that need a countersigned copy can request one from
[privacy@cal.com](mailto:privacy@cal.com); its terms are the same as this page.

Capitalized terms not defined in this DPA have the meaning given in the Agreement.

## 1. Definitions

- "**Data Protection Laws**" means all laws relating to the processing of personal data that apply to a party's
  processing of Customer Personal Data under the Agreement, including, where applicable, the EU General Data Protection
  Regulation 2016/679 ("**GDPR**"), the GDPR as retained in UK law and the UK Data Protection Act 2018 ("**UK GDPR**"),
  the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection ("**FADP**"), and the California Consumer Privacy Act as amended by the
  California Privacy Rights Act ("**CCPA**") and other U.S. state privacy laws.
- "**Customer Personal Data**" means personal data contained in Customer Data that Cal.com processes on behalf of
  Customer in providing the Services.
- "**Subprocessor**" means a third party engaged by Cal.com that processes Customer Personal Data.
- "**Security Incident**" means a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss,
  alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or access to, Customer Personal Data processed by Cal.com or its
  Subprocessors.
- "**Standard Contractual Clauses**" or "**SCCs**" means the standard contractual clauses annexed to European
  Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914.
- "**UK Addendum**" means the International Data Transfer Addendum to the SCCs issued by the UK Information
  Commissioner under section 119A of the UK Data Protection Act 2018.
- "**DPF**" means the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and its UK Extension.
- "controller", "processor", "data subject", "personal data", "processing", "business", "service provider",
  "sale" and "share" have the meanings given in the applicable Data Protection Laws.

## 2. Roles and scope

2.1 **Roles.** For Customer Personal Data, Customer is the controller (or, where Customer acts on behalf of another
controller, a processor) and Cal.com is a processor (or subprocessor) and, under the CCPA, a service provider. Where
Customer is itself a processor, Customer confirms that its controller has authorized its instructions, including the
appointment of Cal.com.

2.2 **Scope.** This DPA does not cover personal data for which Cal.com is a controller, such as account, billing and
security data about Customer's users. That data is described in our [Privacy Policy](/legal/privacy).

2.3 **Details of processing.** The subject matter, nature, purpose and duration of the processing, and the types of
personal data and categories of data subjects, are described in [Annex 1](#annex-1-details-of-processing).

## 3. Customer's obligations

3.1 Customer is responsible for the lawfulness of the processing of Customer Personal Data and of its instructions,
including having a lawful basis (and, where required, consent) for every message it sends and for enabling open and
click tracking, and for giving data subjects the notices that Data Protection Laws require.

3.2 Customer will not submit special categories of personal data, data relating to criminal convictions, protected
health information or payment card data to the Services unless Cal.com has agreed to it in writing.

## 4. Cal.com's obligations

4.1 **Instructions.** Cal.com will process Customer Personal Data only on Customer's documented instructions, unless
Data Protection Laws or other applicable law require otherwise, in which case Cal.com will inform Customer before
processing unless that law prohibits it. The Agreement, this DPA and Customer's use and configuration of the Services
(including through the dashboard and the API) are Customer's complete instructions. Additional instructions require a
written agreement. Cal.com will inform Customer if, in its opinion, an instruction infringes Data Protection Laws.

4.2 **Platform protection.** Customer instructs Cal.com to process Customer Personal Data as needed to protect the
Services, Customer and data subjects, as described in the Agreement and the
[Acceptable Use Policy](/legal/acceptable-use). This includes maintaining suppression lists, screening a sample of
messages for spam, phishing, malware and fraud with automated systems including AI models, and monitoring bounce and
complaint rates.

4.3 **Confidentiality.** Cal.com will ensure that the people it authorizes to process Customer Personal Data are bound
by appropriate obligations of confidentiality and access it only as needed to provide, secure and support the Services.

4.4 **Security.** Cal.com will implement and maintain the technical and organizational measures described in
[Annex 2](#annex-2-technical-and-organizational-measures). Cal.com may update them, provided the updates do not
materially reduce the overall level of protection of Customer Personal Data.

4.5 **CCPA.** As a service provider, Cal.com will not: (a) sell or share Customer Personal Data; (b) retain, use or
disclose it for any purpose other than the business purposes specified in the Agreement, including for any commercial
purpose other than providing the Services; (c) retain, use or disclose it outside the direct business relationship
between Cal.com and Customer; or (d) combine it with personal data Cal.com receives from or on behalf of anyone else,
except as the CCPA permits. Cal.com will comply with the CCPA's obligations applicable to service providers, provide
the same level of privacy protection the CCPA requires, and notify Customer if it can no longer meet its obligations.
Customer may take reasonable and appropriate steps to stop and remediate unauthorized use of Customer Personal Data.
Cal.com certifies that it understands and will comply with these restrictions.

4.6 **Aggregated data.** Nothing in this DPA prevents Cal.com from using aggregated or de-identified data that does not
identify Customer or any individual, as permitted by the Agreement and Data Protection Laws.

## 5. Subprocessors

5.1 **Authorization.** Customer gives Cal.com general authorization to engage Subprocessors. The Subprocessors that
process Customer Personal Data are listed in [Annex 3](#annex-3-subprocessors) and in our Trust Center at
[trust.cal.com](https://trust.cal.com), which Customer authorizes.

5.2 **Obligations.** Cal.com will enter into a written agreement with each Subprocessor that imposes data protection
obligations no less protective than those in this DPA, to the extent applicable to the services the Subprocessor
provides. Cal.com remains liable for its Subprocessors' performance of those obligations.

5.3 **Changes.** Cal.com will give Customer at least 30 days' notice before a new Subprocessor begins processing
Customer Personal Data, by updating the list at [trust.cal.com](https://trust.cal.com) and notifying subscribers to
that list or the email address on Customer's account. Customer may object on reasonable data protection grounds by
writing to [privacy@cal.com](mailto:privacy@cal.com) within that period. The parties will discuss the objection in good
faith. If they cannot resolve it, Customer may terminate the affected Services by written notice, and Cal.com will
refund any prepaid fees for the remainder of the term after termination. Cal.com may replace a Subprocessor with less
notice in an emergency affecting the security or availability of the Services, in which case it will notify Customer
promptly and the objection right still applies.

## 6. International transfers

6.1 **Location.** Cal.com and its Subprocessors process Customer Personal Data mainly in the United States.

6.2 **Transfer mechanisms.** To the extent Customer Personal Data subject to the GDPR, the UK GDPR or the FADP is
transferred to a country without an adequacy decision, the parties rely on the following, in this order:

1. **Data Privacy Framework.** Cal.com is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and the UK Extension, and
   will process such data in accordance with the DPF Principles. Cal.com will notify Customer if it determines it can
   no longer meet its obligations under the DPF.
2. **Standard Contractual Clauses.** Where the DPF does not apply, or ceases to be a valid transfer mechanism, the SCCs
   are incorporated into this DPA as set out in [section 6.3](#6-international-transfers), together with the UK
   Addendum or the Swiss amendments in [section 6.4](#6-international-transfers) where applicable.

6.3 **The SCCs.** Where the SCCs apply:

- Module Two (controller to processor) applies where Customer is a controller, and Module Three (processor to
  processor) where Customer is a processor;
- Clause 7 (docking clause) applies;
- in Clause 9, Option 2 (general written authorization) applies, with the notice period in
  [section 5.3](#5-subprocessors);
- in Clause 11, the optional language does not apply;
- in Clause 17, Option 1 applies, and the SCCs are governed by the law of Ireland;
- in Clause 18(b), disputes are resolved by the courts of Ireland;
- Annex I of the SCCs is completed with [Annex 1](#annex-1-details-of-processing) of this DPA, and the competent
  supervisory authority is the one determined under Clause 13;
- Annex II of the SCCs is completed with [Annex 2](#annex-2-technical-and-organizational-measures); and
- Annex III of the SCCs is completed with [Annex 3](#annex-3-subprocessors).

6.4 **UK and Switzerland.** For transfers subject to the UK GDPR, the UK Addendum applies, completed with the
information in this DPA; Table 4 is completed by selecting "neither party". For transfers subject to the FADP, the SCCs
apply with these amendments: references to the GDPR are to the FADP; the Swiss Federal Data Protection and Information
Commissioner is the competent supervisory authority for such transfers; "Member State" includes Switzerland so that
data subjects in Switzerland can sue in their place of habitual residence; and the SCCs protect the data of legal
entities until the revised FADP no longer does.

6.5 **Precedence.** If there is any conflict between this DPA and the SCCs (or the UK Addendum), the SCCs (or the UK
Addendum) prevail.

## 7. Security Incidents

7.1 Cal.com will notify Customer without undue delay, and in any event within 72 hours, after becoming aware of a
Security Incident. The notice will be sent to the email address of Customer's organization owners and will describe,
to the extent then known, the nature of the incident, the categories and approximate number of data subjects and
records concerned, its likely consequences, and the measures taken or proposed to address it. Where information is not
available at once, Cal.com will provide it in phases as it becomes available.

7.2 Cal.com will promptly take reasonable steps to contain, investigate and mitigate the Security Incident, and provide
reasonable assistance to Customer in meeting its own notification obligations.

7.3 Cal.com's notification of a Security Incident is not an acknowledgment of fault or liability. Unsuccessful attempts
or activities that do not compromise the security of Customer Personal Data, such as pings, port scans, failed sign-in
attempts or denial-of-service attacks, are not Security Incidents.

## 8. Assistance

8.1 **Data subject requests.** Cal.com will provide Customer with the means, through the Services or otherwise, to
respond to requests from data subjects to exercise their rights under Data Protection Laws. If Cal.com receives such a
request directly about Customer Personal Data, it will not respond to it other than to refer the data subject to
Customer, unless legally required, and will promptly forward it to Customer where it can identify Customer.

8.2 **Other assistance.** Taking into account the nature of the processing and the information available to it,
Cal.com will provide reasonable assistance to Customer with data protection impact assessments and prior consultations
with supervisory authorities that relate to Customer's use of the Services.

8.3 **Government requests.** If Cal.com receives a request from a public authority for access to Customer Personal
Data, it will (a) refer the authority to Customer where possible; (b) notify Customer promptly, unless prohibited by
law; (c) review the legality of the request and challenge it where there are reasonable grounds to do so; and (d)
disclose only the minimum amount of data necessary to comply with a lawful request.

## 9. Audits

9.1 On written request, Cal.com will make available to Customer the information reasonably necessary to demonstrate
compliance with this DPA, such as security documentation and the results of third-party assessments, where they exist.
Such information is Cal.com's Confidential Information.

9.2 If that information is not sufficient to demonstrate compliance, or where a supervisory authority or Data
Protection Laws require it, Customer may audit Cal.com's compliance with this DPA, itself or through an independent
auditor bound by confidentiality, no more than once in any 12-month period (except after a Security Incident or at a
supervisory authority's direction). Customer will give at least 30 days' notice, and the parties will agree on the
scope, timing and duration of the audit in advance. Audits will be conducted during business hours, in a way that does
not unreasonably disrupt Cal.com's operations or compromise the security or confidentiality of other customers' data,
and at Customer's expense.

## 10. Deletion and return

10.1 During the term of the Agreement, Customer can export and remove Customer Personal Data through the Services, and
ask Cal.com to delete the rest by writing to [privacy@cal.com](mailto:privacy@cal.com).

10.2 After the Agreement ends, Customer has 30 days to export Customer Personal Data. Cal.com will then delete it from
its active systems within a further 30 days, and from backups as they expire in the normal course, unless applicable
law requires Cal.com to keep it. Data retained under law or in backups remains protected by this DPA and is not
actively processed. On request, Cal.com will confirm deletion in writing.

## 11. Liability, term and precedence

11.1 **Liability.** Each party's liability arising out of or relating to this DPA, including the SCCs to the extent
permitted by them, is subject to the limitations of liability in the Agreement.

11.2 **Term.** This DPA remains in effect for as long as Cal.com processes Customer Personal Data, and its obligations
survive termination of the Agreement until the data is deleted.

11.3 **Precedence.** If there is any conflict between the Agreement and this DPA concerning the processing of Customer
Personal Data, this DPA prevails, subject to [section 6.5](#6-international-transfers).

11.4 **Changes.** Cal.com may update this DPA as required by Data Protection Laws, by a supervisory authority, or to
reflect changes to the Services that do not materially reduce the protection of Customer Personal Data. Other changes
follow the change process in the Terms of Service.

11.5 **Governing law.** This DPA is governed by the law that governs the Agreement, except where the SCCs or Data
Protection Laws require otherwise.

## Annex 2: Technical and organizational measures

**Encryption.** Data is encrypted in transit using TLS, and at rest in Cal.com's databases and storage. Webhook signing
secrets and SMS provider credentials are additionally encrypted with AES-256-GCM. Passwords and API keys are stored only as hashes.

**Access control.** Access to production systems and Customer Personal Data is limited to personnel who need it, on a
least-privilege basis, and is protected by strong authentication. Platform administrators' actions in the Services are
recorded in an append-only audit log.

**Tenant isolation.** Every query against customer data is scoped to the customer's organization. API keys are bound to
a single environment and organization and carry explicit permissions. Each organization's SMS numbers and messages are
kept in its own account with the SMS provider.

**Customer controls.** Customers can require two-factor authentication or single sign-on for their organization,
assign roles to members, revoke sessions and API keys, and separate sandbox from live environments, which never deliver
real messages.

**Data minimization.** Attachment contents are deleted once they are no longer needed for delivery. Tracking records
the user agent but not the IP address. Content screening sends only a bounded extract of a message (sender, subject,
up to 8,000 characters of text and link hosts and paths without query strings; for SMS, the sending number, the text
and link hosts and paths), never recipients or attachments, to a provider that retains nothing; webhook events about
text messages never include their text; screening verdicts never store message content. Rate limiting keys store email
addresses only as hashes. Risk assessments keep email addresses, IP addresses and user agents for 90 days, remember
identifiers (including card fingerprints and device identifiers) across assessments only as keyed hashes, never
store card numbers, look IP addresses up in locally hosted data, and send
domain registries (RDAP) only domain names. Captcha secrets are stored encrypted. Captcha tokens and IP
addresses go to the captcha provider Customer configures, on Customer's instruction. Content classification sends up
to 8,000 characters of the submitted text and link hosts and paths without query strings to a provider that retains
nothing, and keeps only the resulting scores; the text is never stored or logged. Rate limit keys are kept only as
keyed hashes, in a cache, until their bucket refills. Phone checks send the phone data provider only the number,
and only a well-formed one; neither the number nor the answer is stored or logged (answers are held in memory for up
to an hour, so a repeated check isn't sent again).

**Network and application security.** Outbound webhook requests are restricted to public addresses, with protections
against server-side request forgery. Rate limits protect authentication and the API from abuse. Customer-supplied HTML
is displayed in the dashboard only in a sandboxed frame. A content security policy restricts the scripts the website
and dashboard may load.

**Availability and resilience.** The Services run on redundant cloud infrastructure, with regular backups and
monitoring.

**Vulnerability management.** Dependencies are kept up to date, changes are reviewed and tested before release, and
vulnerabilities can be reported through a responsible disclosure program at [cal.com/security](https://cal.com/security).

**Personnel.** Personnel with access to Customer Personal Data are bound by confidentiality obligations.

**Incident response.** Cal.com maintains a process to detect, respond to and notify Security Incidents, as described in
[section 7](#7-security-incidents).

**Subprocessors.** Subprocessors are selected after assessing their security and data protection practices, and are
bound by written agreements (see [section 5](#5-subprocessors)).
