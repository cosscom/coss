---
title: Dedicated IPs
description: Send live email from your own IPs, warmed up automatically.
---

By default, live email leaves from IPs shared with other senders, whose reputation is kept high
for everyone. With the **Dedicated IPs** add-on, your organization's live email leaves from IPs
only it uses, so your deliverability depends on your sending alone. They are Amazon SES managed
dedicated IPs: SES leases them, adds and removes IPs as your volume changes, and warms each one
up per mailbox provider.

Dedicated IPs suit steady volume: SES assigns the first IP once you send a few hundred emails a
day over a few days, and may hand it back if volume stays low (your email then leaves from shared
IPs again, with nothing held back). Below that, shared IPs deliver better.

## Turning them on

Add **Dedicated IPs** on the dashboard's billing page (owners; on the plans listed in
[Add-ons](/docs/billing#add-ons)). Once the payment clears, COSS sets up your pool, usually
within a couple of minutes, and live emails go through it from then on. Your organization needs
at least one sending domain. Sandbox emails are never affected.

The add-on's monthly price is charged up front, prorated for the first month, and email sent
through your dedicated IPs is also metered (see [usage rates](/docs/billing#usage)).

## Transactional and marketing email

Both [streams](/docs/email/streams) leave from your dedicated IPs, so all your live email builds
the same IPs' reputation. Each stream still goes through its own sending configuration: coss.com
measures their bounce and complaint rates apart, and pausing marketing email never stops your
transactional email. Because they share the IPs, though, a marketing campaign that draws many
complaints can hurt how mailbox providers treat your transactional email too: keep your lists
clean.

## Warm-up

New IPs have no reputation, and mailbox providers accept only a little mail from them at first.
SES ramps each IP up per provider as it builds trust: while it does, what an IP can't take yet
leaves from shared IPs, so warm-up never delays your email. The **Dedicated IPs** page in the
dashboard shows how far along it is:

| Status         | Meaning |
| -------------- | ------- |
| `provisioning` | The pool is being set up; email still leaves from shared IPs. |
| `waiting`      | Every pool is taken right now; COSS support has been told. |
| `warming`      | Live email goes through your pool while its IPs warm up. |
| `ready`        | At least 95% of the last 7 days' deliveries (at least 1,000) left from your IPs. |
| `releasing`    | The add-on ended: email is back on shared IPs, and the pool is released a day later. |

A pool can go back from `ready` to `warming`, for example when a sudden rise in volume makes SES
add an IP. Read the same status from the API:

```ts twoslash
import { Coss } from "coss";
const coss = new Coss();
// ---cut---
const { data: pool } = await coss.email.dedicatedIps.get();

console.log(pool?.status, pool?.dedicated_share);
for (const ip of pool?.ips ?? []) console.log(ip.ip, ip.delivered_7d);
```

## Keeping a good reputation

A dedicated IP's reputation is yours alone, good or bad. Keep bounce and complaint rates low
(the [Reputation](/docs/email/delivery) guard watches them either way), send to people who asked
for your email, and change volume gradually. If one of your IPs is listed on a blocklist, ask that
list for its removal: SES doesn't rotate managed IPs.

## Removing them

Remove the add-on on the billing page: it ends with the billing month. Live email then goes back
to shared IPs at once, and the pool is released a day later; adding it back before then keeps the
same IPs.
