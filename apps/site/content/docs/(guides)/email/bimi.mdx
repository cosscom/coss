---
title: Brand logos (BIMI)
description: Show your logo next to your emails in Gmail, Apple Mail and other inboxes.
---

BIMI (Brand Indicators for Message Identification) lets mailbox providers show your logo next to
the emails you send. It is set up entirely in your own DNS: a record that points at your logo and at
a certificate proving the logo is yours. COSS doesn't host logos or issue certificates. It checks
your DNS and tells you what is still missing.

## What you need

1. **A verified domain.** COSS signs your emails with DKIM as your domain, so they pass DMARC once
   the domain is [verified](/docs/email/domains).
2. **DMARC at enforcement.** Your domain's DMARC policy must be `p=quarantine` or `p=reject`, and
   apply to all of your mail: no `pct` below 100, no `t=y`. A domain without a DMARC record of its
   own uses its parent's, and its `sp` (subdomain policy) applies when set, so `sp=none` there
   doesn't count either. For a subdomain, the organizational domain's own policy (`p`, on
   `example.com` for `mail.example.com`) must be at enforcement too, for all of its mail. Before you tighten the policy, read your DMARC reports (`rua=`) to make sure
   every service that sends as your domain passes DMARC. Then move from `p=none` to `quarantine` to
   `reject`.
3. **Your logo as an SVG** in the SVG Tiny Portable/Secure (SVG Tiny PS) profile. It should be
   square, with a solid background, and served over HTTPS. Mailbox providers suggest keeping it under
   32 KB.
4. **A mark certificate** for that logo, as a PEM file served over HTTPS. Gmail and Apple Mail show
   logos only with one, and you buy it from a certificate authority approved by the
   [BIMI Group](https://bimigroup.org/):
   - A **Verified Mark Certificate (VMC)** needs the logo to be a registered trademark. Gmail and
     Apple Mail both accept it, and Gmail also shows a verified checkmark.
   - A **Common Mark Certificate (CMC)** needs no trademark, but the logo must have been shown on your
     website for at least 12 months. Gmail accepts it; Apple Mail does not.

Other mailbox providers have their own requirements. Each provider validates the logo and
certificate itself and decides when to show the logo (sender reputation and volume play a part),
and caches what it finds.

## The record

Publish a `TXT` record at `default._bimi.` followed by your domain:

| Type  | Name                       | Value                                                                              |
| ----- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `TXT` | `default._bimi.example.com` | `v=BIMI1; l=https://example.com/brand/logo.svg; a=https://example.com/brand/vmc.pem` |

`l=` is your logo's URL and `a=` your certificate's. Keep exactly one `v=BIMI1` record at the name:
mailbox providers ignore the domain's logo when there are several.

Mailbox providers look up the record for the domain of the From address. If there is none, they
use the organizational domain's: emails from `@mail.example.com` show the logo of
`default._bimi.example.com` unless `mail.example.com` has a record of its own. COSS sends with the
`default` selector.

## Checking it

COSS checks the BIMI record and the DMARC policy with the rest of the domain's DNS records. That
happens every 12 hours once the domain is verified, or right away with **Verify now** on the
domain's page in the dashboard (**Domains**) or `coss.domains.verify(id)`. The result is on the
domain's page under **Brand logo (BIMI)**, and in the domain's `bimi` field:

```ts twoslash
import { Coss } from "coss";
const coss = new Coss();
declare const domainId: string;
// ---cut---
const { data: domain } = await coss.domains.get(domainId);

console.log(domain?.bimi.status); // "configured"
for (const issue of domain?.bimi.issues ?? []) {
  console.log(issue.code, issue.message);
}
```

| Status        | Meaning                                                                                       |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `configured`  | The record is valid and DMARC is enforced. Mailbox providers can find the logo and certificate. |
| `invalid`     | A record exists, but something keeps the logo from showing: see `issues`.                     |
| `missing`     | There is no BIMI record. `issues` still lists what DMARC needs first.                         |
| `not_checked` | DNS hasn't been checked yet, or couldn't be reached during the last check.                    |

`bimi` also has the record found (`name`, `record`), its `logo_url` and `certificate_url`, and
`dmarc_policy`, the DMARC policy that applies to the domain. These are the issues it can report:

| Issue                   | Meaning                                                                         |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `dmarc_missing`         | The domain has no valid DMARC record (one listing a tag twice is invalid), or more than one. |
| `dmarc_not_enforced`    | The DMARC policy is `p=none` (or `sp=none` for a subdomain), or the organizational domain's `p` is `none`. |
| `dmarc_partial`         | The DMARC policy (or the organizational domain's) applies to only part of the mail (`pct` below 100, or `t=y`). |
| `record_multiple`       | There is more than one `v=BIMI1` record.                                        |
| `record_invalid`        | The record doesn't start with `v=BIMI1`, or lists a tag more than once.          |
| `logo_missing`          | The record has no logo URL (`l=`).                                              |
| `logo_not_https`        | The logo URL isn't an `https://` URL.                                           |
| `certificate_missing`   | The record has no certificate URL (`a=`).                                       |
| `certificate_not_https` | The certificate URL isn't an `https://` URL.                                    |

COSS checks DNS only: it never downloads your logo or certificate. A `configured` domain can still
go without a logo if the certificate has expired, doesn't cover the domain, or doesn't match the
logo. Your certificate authority can check those for you.
